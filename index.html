How it was checked. Every hourly rate the page uses reproduces the award's own Schedule B figures to the cent. The calculation engine runs about 1,800 automated checks every time the page is built - rates, holiday calendars, classifications, and that a dearer setting never prices lower - and the method was reviewed clause by clause against the award text, with independent recalculations, before release.
What this is. The award floor for the role and roster described - the least the business can lawfully pay under this award - not a recommended offer and not legal advice. It prices the adult rate under this award with no enterprise agreement. That is also the minimum for anyone under 21 who works the bar - the FOH Attendant role, or anyone who makes or serves drinks or changes kegs - because junior employees working as liquor service employees must be paid as adults (clause 13.5); a 20-year-old gets the adult rate in any role (Table 4). Only a junior under 20 who never works the bar, such as a kitchen hand, may be paid the Table 4 percentages (85% at 19, 70% at 18, 60% at 17, 50% under 17), applied to the hourly rates in Schedule B.2. A salary set from this page also needs: a written annualised wage agreement stating the salary, the clauses it covers and the outer limits (clause 20.1(d)), with a copy to the employee and kept as a time and wages record (clause 20.1(e)); start and finish times and unpaid breaks recorded and signed or acknowledged by the employee each pay period or roster cycle (clause 20.2(c)); and a comparison every 12 months, and when employment or the agreement ends, with any shortfall paid within 14 days (clause 20.2(b)).
